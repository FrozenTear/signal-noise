# Apple Sues OpenAI, Alleging Former Employee Exploited Authentication Bug to Steal Hardware Secrets

## Summary

Apple filed a federal trade-secret lawsuit on July 10, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, naming OpenAI, its hardware subsidiary io Products, and two former Apple employees as defendants. At the center of the filing is Chang Liu, a former Apple system electrical engineer who Apple alleges exploited a rare authentication vulnerability to download more than a thousand pages of confidential hardware documents weeks after joining OpenAI. The suit also names Tang Tan, a former Apple hardware executive who now leads OpenAI's device division, alleging he directed departing Apple staff to bring proprietary parts and prototypes to job interviews.

## Body

Apple filed suit in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California on July 10, 2026, accusing OpenAI, its hardware-focused subsidiary io Products, and two former Apple employees of misappropriating confidential trade secrets.

The allegations at the core of the case concern Chang Liu, who Apple describes as a former system electrical engineer. According to Apple's complaint, Liu exploited a "rare, previously unknown authentication bug" — a zero-day vulnerability in Apple's internal network authentication system — to continue accessing Apple's internal file storage weeks after departing for OpenAI in early 2026. Apple alleges Liu used this access to download more than a thousand pages of confidential hardware documents, including technical manufacturing specifications, engineering presentations, and data on unreleased products.

The specific technical mechanism of the authentication vulnerability is not disclosed in the filing. Apple says it discovered and patched the bug after detecting Liu's access, and that Liu appears to have been the only person who exploited it — though the underlying flaw theoretically exposed other accounts as well.

Apple's complaint includes what it characterizes as a contemporaneous message Liu allegedly sent to a colleague after discovering the continued access: "LOL, I found out I can access the [network storage], so funny." Apple also alleges Liu failed to return a company-issued laptop.

The second named individual is Tang Tan, a former Apple vice president of product design who now leads hardware efforts at OpenAI, including at the Jony Ive-connected io Products initiative. Apple alleges Tan engaged in a distinct category of conduct: using confidential Apple project codenames during recruiting interviews with Apple employees, directing candidates to bring "actual parts" and prototypes to OpenAI job interviews for "show and tell" sessions, and distributing internal Apple security documents to new OpenAI hires. Apple characterizes Tan's conduct as a pattern of circumventing its standard departure security protocols.

The complaint names io Products alongside OpenAI as a defendant, consistent with Tan's role bridging both organizations.

Apple says it raised concerns with OpenAI in February 2026 and received no response before filing suit. The company seeks injunctive relief and damages.

OpenAI has previously stated it has "no interest in other companies' trade secrets." The company has not issued a statement directly addressing the specific allegations in Apple's complaint. Neither Tan nor Liu has issued a public response.

The suit arrives against the backdrop of significant talent overlap between the two companies. More than 400 former Apple employees now work at OpenAI, according to 9to5Mac — a figure that reflects the scale of OpenAI's recent hardware and device ambitions. Jony Ive, Evans Hankey, and Scott Cannon are not personally named in the filing.

All allegations in Apple's complaint are untested legal claims by one party in an active lawsuit.
