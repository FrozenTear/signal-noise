# EU competition chief puts "entire AI stack" in the antitrust perimeter

**By Priya Nair | Tech | 2026-06-04**

Three months after Teresa Ribera told a Berlin conference that the EU is scrutinising the "entire AI stack," the Commission's competition chief said on 2 June 2026 that EU powers to intervene speedily during a probe are "increasingly important." The remark is not a case announcement. It is a signal that the doctrine declared in March is still being pressed.

---

At the 23rd International Conference on Competition (IKK) in Berlin on 12 March 2026, Ribera — Executive Vice-President for a Clean, Just and Competitive Transition and the Commission's lead on competition policy — framed the scope of EU scrutiny in terms that went beyond any individual product or platform. The Commission, she said, is "looking at the entire AI stack": AI models and chatbots, the training data that produces them, cloud infrastructure, and the energy resources that power the compute.

That phrase is the verbatim record. Bloomberg's headline framing — "Big Tech's 'Entire' AI Operations" — is the wire desk's render, not Ribera's words. The distinction matters because "stack" carries a specific technical meaning: a vertical integration of interdependent components, each of which gates access to the layers above and below it.

EU antitrust enforcement has spent roughly a decade at the application layer: app stores, search defaults, browser choice screens, interoperability mandates. The integrated-supply-chain framing Ribera signalled at the IKK points at a different theory of harm. Chips feed cloud compute; cloud compute enables model training at scale; scale in training data compounds into capability advantages that are difficult to replicate without equivalent infrastructure access. The Commission is saying, in public, that this is the structure it is looking at — not just the products that sit on top.

---

**Four concrete investigations are already running. Anchor here; do not speculate beyond them.**

**Meta / WhatsApp AI access.** On 4 December 2025, the Commission opened a formal antitrust investigation into Meta's October 2025 WhatsApp Business policy. Meta had restricted third-party AI providers — named in reporting as including OpenAI and Perplexity — from accessing the WhatsApp Business infrastructure. The probe covers the EEA except Italy, where a parallel national investigation is proceeding independently. This is at investigation stage: no Statement of Objections has been issued.

**Nvidia bundling.** An informal probe is examining whether Nvidia ties GPU sales to networking equipment. EU regulators have cited approximately 84 percent as Nvidia's share of the AI-GPU market. Informal means the matter has not been upgraded to a formal proceedings notice; the designation matters for what documents the Commission can compel.

**Nvidia / Run:ai acquisition.** The Commission is reviewing Nvidia's acquisition of Run:ai following an Article 22 referral by Italy. Article 22 of the EU Merger Regulation allows member states to ask the Commission to examine deals that fall below formal notification thresholds. A call-in for review followed the Italian referral.

**DMA cloud market investigations.** The Commission is examining whether AWS and Microsoft Azure meet the threshold for designation as "core platform services" under the Digital Markets Act. Designation would trigger ex-ante obligations — around interoperability, data access, and self-preferencing — that apply before any finding of abuse.

None of these has produced a Statement of Objections. Framing any of them as an imminent fine or enforcement action would misstate where proceedings stand.

---

Ribera also met the CEOs of Alphabet, Meta, OpenAI, and Amazon during a US visit in March 2026. Those meetings were framed publicly around AI competition discussions, not prior enforcement matters.

One distinction to preserve in any reading of this story: competition law and AI regulation are separate legal instruments. The EU AI Act is sector regulation, already in force, administered through a separate institutional chain under the AI Office. DG COMP — Ribera's directorate — uses antitrust tools under Articles 101 and 102 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, plus the DMA's ex-ante framework. The two can overlap in their targets, but their mechanisms, timelines, and remedies are distinct. Coverage that blurs the two makes it harder to track which enforcement lever is actually moving.

The honest read on the IKK speech: it is a doctrine statement. The Commission has now said, in a named speech, that AI competition scrutiny runs from the chip level to the consumer-facing model. Whether that doctrine translates into case volume — at what pace, against which companies, with what legal theories — is the open question. The 2 June remarks on speedy intervention suggest the Commission's stated appetite for faster action is, at minimum, on the record.
