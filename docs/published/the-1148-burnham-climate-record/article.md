# From Fossil Fuel Treaty to Rosebank: Andy Burnham's Climate Record, in His Own Words

When Andy Burnham endorsed the Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty in June 2025, he used the word "lifeline." When asked twelve months later about reversing Labour's manifesto pledge to stop new North Sea drilling licences, he described having "something of an open mind." Carbon Brief has compiled 28 of his on-record statements on climate, net-zero, and fossil fuels, spanning 2008 to 2026. The distance between those two formulations is where UK climate policy now lives.

Burnham takes office today as the UK's prime minister, replacing Keir Starmer after a Labour leadership contest in which he secured the backing of approximately 322 MPs. His climate record — assembled over almost two decades as a Treasury minister, shadow home secretary, and Mayor of Greater Manchester — is long and public. It is also internally contradictory in one specific, consequential way.

## The structural argument: net-zero as reindustrialisation

Burnham's net-zero framing is regional and economic before it is environmental. At COP26 in Glasgow in November 2021, he told ITV: "The drive to net-zero is a chance to re-industrialise the north of England, this time in a clean way." The argument — that decarbonisation is a jobs programme for post-industrial northern England — runs consistently through his record as mayor. In Greater Manchester he developed it into policy: plans for 30,000 net-zero social rented homes (June 2022), a combined-authority net-zero target aligned to the 2038 benchmark, and the C40 Fossil-Fuel-Free Streets Declaration he signed in 2018.

By April 2025, the framing had shifted register. At the Innovation Zero World Congress in London, he said: "An opportunity is opening up for Britain as other countries move away from net-zero." That is a different argument — treating net-zero commitment as a competitive differentiator rather than a regional obligation. Both arguments are compatible with reducing emissions. They are not the same political test.

## The licensing distinction that his "open mind" concerns

Labour's 2024 manifesto committed to ending new oil and gas exploration licences in the North Sea. The Rosebank and Jackdaw fields were licensed under the previous Conservative government; they have not been developed. Under the terms of the manifesto pledge, fields already licensed under a predecessor government are not new licences. Approving them would not technically breach the commitment.

This is the specific mechanism Burnham's June 2026 "open mind" statement — reported in the New Statesman — addresses. It is not a position on fossil fuels in general. It is a position on whether fields already licensed but not yet producing should proceed to production.

The climate calculus on that question differs from the licence question. Rosebank is the largest undeveloped field on the UK continental shelf, with recoverable oil estimated at approximately 300 million barrels; the North Sea Transition Authority approved its development. Jackdaw is a significant undeveloped gas field. If either is developed and its output is combusted, the associated emissions occur regardless of the licensing label attached at extraction. The distinction matters for the political pledge. It does not materially affect the physical outcome in the atmosphere.

Scope 3 emissions — those arising from the burning of oil and gas extracted from these fields, wherever that combustion occurs — are the relevant accounting frame. Burnham's public record does not include a stated Scope 3 accounting position on North Sea extraction. That absence is not supplied here.

## The temporal arc of the record

Carbon Brief's compilation spans 2008 to 2026. The early entries are parliamentary and campaign statements establishing a baseline climate-action position — including a January 2008 House of Commons speech on the economic impacts of climate change, delivered during his final days as chief secretary to the Treasury, and a July 2015 Labour leadership bid commitment: "Labour under my leadership will never turn our back on either our duty to tackle climate change or the prospects offered by the green economy." The middle period (2018–2022) shows the strongest operational commitments: the C40 streets declaration, the biodiversity emergency declaration for Greater Manchester (March 2022), the net-zero social housing plan.

The June 2025 fossil fuel treaty endorsement sits in the activist-mayor register: "I am proud to endorse the fossil-fuel treaty proposal today on behalf of Greater Manchester... there should be no turning away from net-zero."

The 2026 leadership campaign statements occupy a different register. His April 2025 Innovation Zero speech observed that "an opportunity is opening up for Britain as other countries move away from net-zero" — a framing that treats net-zero commitment as a competitive differentiator rather than an obligation. His June 2026 New Statesman comments on North Sea drilling extended that logic into permissibility language.

This is not a repudiation of net-zero — Burnham has made no such statement, and there is none in the 28-quote record. It is a shift in the direction of pragmatic accommodation. Whether that accommodation is rhetorical — addressing Scottish Labour MPs and workers in fossil-fuel-adjacent industries — or substantive — a genuine policy move toward approving Rosebank and Jackdaw — is the first question his energy secretary appointment will answer. He has not named that minister yet.

## What the record does not resolve

The 28 quotes do not include: a stated Scope 3 accounting position; a timeline for the net-zero transition benchmarked against the UK's legally binding 2050 target or the 2035 Nationally Determined Contribution submitted under the Paris Agreement; a view on the UK Emissions Trading Scheme or the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism; a position on interconnect infrastructure or capacity market reform, which constrain how quickly offshore wind capacity translates into delivered power; or a stated framework for the North Sea Transition Authority's role under a new licensing environment.

These are the mechanisms where headline commitments meet implementation.
